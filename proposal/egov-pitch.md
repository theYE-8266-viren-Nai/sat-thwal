# Set Thwal E-Government Pivot Pitch

## Positioning

Set Thwal is a UIT-first, school-centralized student welfare system for coordinating verified services around student life. The platform gives the university administration a single operating view for student verification, approved service providers, welfare requests, and request outcomes across tutoring, hostels, food, and transportation.

Instead of presenting Set Thwal as an open marketplace, this proposal positions it as public-service infrastructure: a trusted digital channel where eligible UIT students can ask for help, school administrators can oversee demand, and only approved providers can respond.

## One-Line Pitch

Set Thwal helps UIT administer student welfare requests through verified students, approved providers, and school-supervised service coordination.

## Proposal Abstract

Set Thwal is a UIT-first e-government pilot for student welfare coordination. The system gives the school a supervised digital channel where eligible students can request support for tutoring, accommodation, food, and transportation, while administrators verify access, approve providers, and monitor request outcomes.

The pilot is designed to replace fragmented informal coordination with a school-governed service workflow. Students gain a trusted place to request help, providers participate only after approval, and UIT receives visibility into welfare demand, unresolved requests, and provider responsiveness. By centering verification, approval, and reporting, Set Thwal can serve as a practical model for university-level digital welfare administration.

## Problem

Students often need support outside the classroom: safe housing, reliable transportation, affordable food, and academic help. These needs are usually handled through scattered chats, informal referrals, phone calls, and manual administrator follow-up. That makes it difficult for the school to know who is requesting support, which providers are trustworthy, whether a request was handled, and where student welfare pressure is increasing.

For administrators, the challenge is not only matching supply and demand. It is accountability. UIT needs a controlled system that verifies students, approves providers, supervises requests, and produces evidence that student needs are being handled through an orderly public-service process.

## Proposed System

Set Thwal becomes a school-controlled e-government student welfare system with UIT as the first deployment.

Students sign up and verify their UIT identity before accessing the service. Providers are not automatically published; they are reviewed and approved by school administrators before students can request their services. Every request creates an auditable record that administrators can monitor by status, category, provider, and student need.

The system supports four welfare categories:

- Academic support through approved tutors.
- Safe accommodation through approved hostel providers.
- Affordable food access through approved restaurant or meal providers.
- Campus transportation through approved routes and drivers.

## Core System Features

- Verified student access: students enter the welfare channel through school-linked identity verification.
- Approved provider participation: tutors, hostel owners, food providers, and transportation providers are reviewed before their services are visible.
- School-supervised request lifecycle: requests move through visible statuses such as pending, confirmed, completed, or cancelled.
- Category-level welfare coordination: tutoring, hostels, food, and transportation are handled as public-service support categories instead of unrelated marketplaces.
- Admin oversight and reporting: administrators can monitor demand, provider responsiveness, unresolved cases, and service coverage.
- Pilot-ready governance: the first deployment stays focused on UIT so the school can validate policy, workflow, and reporting before wider replication.

## Administrative Control

The school administration remains the center of authority. Admin users can review student verification signals, approve or reject providers, inspect provider payment or registration status, monitor service listings, and oversee student requests from submission through completion.

This structure gives UIT a practical governance layer:

- Student access is tied to UIT verification.
- Provider access is permissioned, not open.
- Service publication depends on approval.
- Requests are visible to administrators.
- Completion status can be tracked instead of lost in informal channels.

## How the Pilot Works

1. A student creates an account and completes UIT student verification.
2. The verified student browses approved welfare support categories.
3. The student submits a request for a tutor, hostel, food package, or transportation route.
4. The request is recorded with a trackable status and becomes visible in the relevant oversight workflow.
5. The approved provider responds to the request within their own service area.
6. Administrators monitor pending, confirmed, cancelled, completed, and unresolved requests.
7. UIT uses the collected demand and status data to improve welfare planning and provider supervision.

## Student Verification

The platform should prioritize verified UIT students. Student ID verification creates a trusted boundary around welfare access and reduces misuse by non-students or unverified accounts.

Verification also improves public-service reporting. The school can distinguish real student demand from general public traffic and can make better decisions about transportation coverage, hostel shortages, food affordability, and tutoring support.

## Approved Provider Network

Set Thwal should only surface providers that meet administrative approval requirements. Providers can include tutors, hostel owners, restaurant operators, and transportation drivers or route operators.

An approved-provider model protects students and protects the school. It creates a clear standard for who is allowed to serve students through the system, gives administrators a review point before listings go live, and supports future policies such as documentation checks, renewal, complaint handling, and provider suspension.

## Request Oversight

Every request should be treated as a welfare coordination record. Administrators need visibility into pending, confirmed, completed, rejected, or unresolved requests.

This oversight layer allows UIT to:

- Identify categories with high student demand.
- See which providers are responsive.
- Track whether approved services are actually serving students.
- Escalate issues when requests remain unresolved.
- Use real demand patterns for planning and reporting.

## Public-Service Value

Set Thwal is valuable because it converts fragmented student support into a transparent school service. The platform can help UIT deliver faster coordination, safer provider access, better student protection, and clearer evidence of welfare activity.

For students, the value is trust and convenience. For providers, the value is formal access to eligible students through a recognized school channel. For administrators, the value is control, oversight, and data for better public-service decisions.

## Pilot Scope

The first deployment should remain UIT-first and focused. A narrow pilot can validate:

- UIT student verification.
- Admin approval of providers.
- Request submission and status tracking.
- Admin dashboards for demand and provider oversight.
- Reporting across tutors, hostels, food, and transportation.

Once the UIT model is stable, the same structure can be adapted for other universities or education departments without changing the core governance idea: school-administered welfare coordination.

## Out of Scope for This Pitch

This pitch does not require changing the application workflow during the proposal stage. The focus is the policy and narrative pivot: Set Thwal should be described as a school-supervised e-government/student welfare pilot rather than a general commercial marketplace.

## Closing Message

Set Thwal is not just a convenience app. It is a school-centered digital welfare channel that helps UIT verify students, approve trusted providers, supervise service requests, and deliver visible public-service value.
